Long AI conversations have a quiet failure mode: the model slowly forgets what you told it.

This week I fixed that in Model Council, my local-first app that asks multiple AI models the same question and lets a "chair" synthesize the best answer.

The problem: conversation memory was clipping every answer to 500 characters and dropping anything older than 8 rounds. Resume a long session, and the model got fragments — not context.

The fix: automatic compaction. When a chat grows too long, a local LLM merges the older rounds into a rolling summary — decisions, facts, preferences, open questions. The newest rounds stay verbatim. The summary survives even after the raw history is trimmed.

The best part: it runs entirely on Ollama, on your own machine. No cloud call, no API cost, no data leaving your laptop.

One rule I held onto: compaction is best-effort. If the local model fails, the conversation continues normally. Memory should degrade gracefully — never take the app down with it.

Small feature, big difference in how "heard" the model feels.
